Understand the engineering model

Supported, not floatingThe beam rests on a pin at A and a roller at B. Each bearing transfers force to its concrete foundation. A pin permits rotation; it is not a built-in, moment-resisting end. With only vertical applied loads, the horizontal reaction is zero. Support reference.
Static equilibriumThere is no resultant force and no resultant moment. Use forces in kN and lengths in m; moments are then in kN·m. ΣFx=0ΣFy=0ΣM=0
A full-span uniform loadReplace a uniformly distributed load w over length L by a resultant W = wL at midspan. Keep full precision until the final answer. The stated UDL is the entire distributed load included in that question. Distributed-load reference.
Two-cable suspensionTwo cables meet at the pin joint J. Their ends attach to bolted overhead anchors. The overhead slab is connected to columns and foundations. Angles are measured from the horizontal. Cable self-weight is neglected; the given suspended load includes its lifting attachment.
Four legs, two symmetric pairsThe load acts on the longitudinal centreline of a rigid, level platform. The four legs are identical, symmetrically positioned and all in contact with the floor. The left legs are A and D; the right legs are B and C. Front–rear symmetry gives equal forces within each pair. The original platform questions neglect platform self-weight. Where a deck weight G is stated, include it at the centre. Only vertical leg forces enter this simplified calculation.
Read the distance originSome dimensions start at A, some start at B, and some are consecutive segments. For platform questions, the given distance may start at either end. Use the labelled dimensions, not a ruler. Cable angles agree with the cable geometry; hardware sizes and the platform perspective are illustrative.
Assembly and force overlayThe assembly view shows the installed structure. The overlay adds reaction or tension arrows without removing its supports. It is not a separate free-body diagram. Solid equipment drawings represent the applied loads, not additional unknown loads.
Triangular and trapezoidal loadingA triangular load is uniformly varying, not uniformly distributed. Its equivalent force is the triangular area and acts one-third of the loaded length from the larger end. A trapezoid can be split into a rectangle and a triangle, each with its own equivalent force. Any offset of the loaded region must be added when measuring from A. Area and centroid reference.
Applied moment, support moment and bending momentThe net external moment on a complete equilibrium system is zero. A question asking for the moment of the applied loads only deliberately excludes the support reactions. A built-in end can provide a resisting moment. A section calculation asks for the internal bending moment at the marked cut, not a reaction at a support.
Known geometry; linear calculations onlyThe additional questions use fixed lengths and positions, areas of rectangles and triangles, trigonometry and linear equilibrium. No question asks you to solve a quadratic equation, integrate a load function, analyse an indeterminate frame or find an unknown span under a distributed load. Triangular and trapezoidal profiles are original teaching extensions, not a claim about a particular examination question.
Overhangs and self-weightAn overhanging beam has a section beyond its bearing; use the bearing spacing in the reaction moment equation. Every generated bearing remains in compression. A stated full-length UDL includes beam self-weight. Other isolated load cases explicitly neglect it. Added platform-weight and rigging questions show the extra weight separately.
Equivalent-load viewThis optional view replaces the distributed load by its equivalent force or forces. Do not count both the original load profile and its replacement. Unknown resultant magnitudes remain labelled with a question mark. Supports remain visible; this is an explanatory overlay, not a detached free-body diagram.
Practice that responds to your answersIncorrect work leads to a fresh calculation of the same type. Repeated difficulty brings a first-step prompt. Independent success unlocks slightly richer loading arrangements, with earlier skills revisited. Detailed progression and starting-point settings are in Teacher view, not on the question screen.
Practice markingThe calculated answer is rounded to 2 decimal places. The inclusive acceptance band is 1.00 below to 1.00 above this answer in the displayed unit (kN or kN·m), checked in 0.01 steps. Whole numbers do not need a decimal suffix. This educational marking allowance is not a construction tolerance, safety factor or permissible design error.
